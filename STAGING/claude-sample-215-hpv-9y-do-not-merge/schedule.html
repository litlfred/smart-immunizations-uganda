    The Uganda routine immunisation schedule, as published by the Ministry of Health and presented by UNEPI. Ages are the scheduled ages; every dose is given at that age or as soon as possible thereafter (see catch-up rules).

    Children

    Age Vaccines Diseases prevented
    Birth BCG, hepatitis B birth dose, OPV0 Tuberculosis, hepatitis B, polio
    6 weeks (1.5 months) OPV1, IPV1, DTP-HepB-Hib 1, PCV1, Rotavirus 1 Polio, diphtheria, whooping cough, tetanus, hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, pneumonia, meningitis, rotavirus diarrhoea
    10 weeks (2.5 months) OPV2, DTP-HepB-Hib 2, PCV2, Rotavirus 2 as at 6 weeks
    14 weeks (3.5 months) OPV3, IPV2, DTP-HepB-Hib 3, PCV3, Rotavirus 3 as at 6 weeks
    6 months Malaria 1, Vitamin A Malaria
    7 months Malaria 2 Malaria
    8 months Malaria 3 Malaria
    9 months Measles-rubella 1 (MR1), Yellow fever Measles, rubella, yellow fever
    12 months Vitamin A, deworming –
    18 months Measles-rubella 2 (MR2), Malaria 4, Vitamin A Measles, rubella, malaria
    24 months Vitamin A –
    9 years (girls) HPV, 1 dose Cancer of the cervix

    Vitamin A (6, 12, 18 and 24 months) and deworming (12 months) appear on the UNEPI schedule but are not vaccines; they are not part of the scheduling logic in this version (see open questions).

    The UNEPI schedule marks the hepatitis B birth dose, IPV1, the third rotavirus dose, yellow fever and MR2 as newly introduced, and malaria and HPV as yet to be introduced at the time of the presentation. The Ministry of Health poster includes malaria and single-dose HPV in the routine schedule.

    Women of child-bearing age (15 to 49 years)

    Dose When Vaccine
    Td1 At first contact Tetanus-diphtheria
    Td2 1 month after Td1 Tetanus-diphtheria
    Td3 6 months after Td2 Tetanus-diphtheria
    Td4 12 months after Td3 Tetanus-diphtheria
    Td5 12 months after Td4 Tetanus-diphtheria

    Diseases targeted

    UNEPI targets 14 vaccine-preventable diseases: tuberculosis, polio, diphtheria, pertussis (whooping cough), tetanus, hepatitis B, Haemophilus influenzae type b, pneumococcal disease, rotavirus gastroenteritis, measles, rubella, yellow fever, cancer of the cervix (HPV) and malaria.

    Messages to caregivers (Ministry of Health poster)

    1. Ask your health worker for your child’s next immunisation date.
    2. Keep your child’s immunisation card safe and bring it every time you visit the health centre.
    3. Report any side effects immediately to the health worker for proper clinical care.
    4. Vaccines are safe, effective and free at all government health facilities.

    Ministry of Health toll-free line: 0800 100 066.
